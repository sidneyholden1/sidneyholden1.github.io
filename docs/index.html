I am a Flatiron Research Fellow in the Flatiron Institute’s Center for Computational Biology, where I am part of the Biological Transport Networks Group. I received my PhD from the University of Edinburgh in 2024, working with Geoff Vasil. I am interested in a variety of problems in physical networks and pattern formation. I approach these problems through a combination of PDE modeling and numerical simulation.

Research

Continuum theories for quantum graphs

Dense networks span multiple scales. We develop continuum descriptions that connect microscopic network geometry to macroscopic differential equations. The aim is to understand how waves and diffusion on a network emerge as effective behavior across the space it occupies.

Read the preprint ↗

Network formation

Transport networks adapt and remodel depending on the flow they carry. We study how the feedback between flow and conductivity can create spatial structure. Our recent preprint identifies a finite-wavelength instability in a continuum model of adaptive transport. It provides a physical mechanism for the joint selection of transport capacity and network density.

Read the preprint ↗

Numerical methods

To enable large, general PDE solving on networks, we are developing a distributed solver for differential equations on physical networks in Dedalus. We develop a distributional calculus for differential equations on edges and coupling conditions at the vertices. This work connects mathematical models to simulations on larger networks, including nonlinear wave dynamics and biological transport.
